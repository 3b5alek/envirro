Radiation: Invisible but Dangerous Energy

Learn about the sources, risks, and safety of radiation exposure.

Understanding Radiation Hazards

Radiation is energy emitted from unstable atoms in the form of particles or electromagnetic waves. It can occur naturally, such as from the sun or radioactive elements in the Earth, or artificially through nuclear power, medical treatments, and weapons.

Exposure to high levels of radiation can be extremely harmful, leading to radiation sickness, genetic damage, cancer, or even death. Accidents at nuclear facilities, such as Chernobyl or Fukushima, have demonstrated the potential dangers of uncontrolled radiation.

Radiation hazards may result from industrial accidents, nuclear weapon detonation, or improper disposal of radioactive materials. Protective clothing, evacuation plans, and sheltering procedures are key to reducing exposure during such events.

Radiation is monitored using detectors, dosimeters, and satellites. Experts continuously assess radiation levels and issue reports to keep the public informed. Rapid response and communication are critical in limiting the impact of radiation-related incidents.
