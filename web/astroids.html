Understanding the Asteroid Threat

Asteroids are rocky objects that orbit the Sun, mostly found in the asteroid belt between Mars and Jupiter. While many remain in stable orbits, some can enter trajectories that bring them close to Earth, posing potential impact threats.

Most asteroids are relatively small, but even a modest-sized asteroid can cause significant damage if it strikes Earth. Past impacts have created large craters and contributed to mass extinctions, highlighting the importance of monitoring near-Earth objects (NEOs).

An asteroid impact could lead to shockwaves, fires, tsunamis, or atmospheric effects depending on its size and location. Early detection and deflection strategies are vital to preventing catastrophic consequences.

Scientists use telescopes, radar systems, and space missions to detect, track, and study asteroids. International space agencies maintain watchlists of potentially hazardous asteroids and publish expert reports to inform the public and plan defense efforts if needed.
