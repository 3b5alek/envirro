Understanding Tsunami Hazards

Tsunamis are a series of large ocean waves typically caused by undersea earthquakes, volcanic eruptions, or landslides. These waves can travel at jetliner speeds across vast distances and unleash devastating power upon reaching coastlines.

A tsunami may start as a barely noticeable wave in deep ocean but grows in height as it approaches shallow waters, leading to massive flooding, destruction, and loss of life in coastal areas. Warning signs may include sudden water recession or ground shaking near the shore.

Tsunami impacts include flooding, infrastructure damage, contamination of freshwater supplies, and long-term displacement of affected populations. Quick evacuation and awareness of natural warning signs are critical for survival.

Tsunami detection systems use seismic sensors, ocean buoys, and satellite data to monitor underwater activity. Agencies issue expert reports and alerts that help coastal communities prepare and evacuate when needed, reducing casualties.
